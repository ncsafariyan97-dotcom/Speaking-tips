The 3 Parts

Part 1 · 4–5 min

Introduction & Interview

Familiar, everyday topics:

🏠 Hometown💼 Work 📚 Studies🎨 Hobbies 👨‍👩‍👧 Family🌅 Daily routines

✍️ Your job: natural, developed answers — aim for 2–3 sentences.

Part 2 · 3–4 min

Individual Long Turn

  • You receive a cue card with a topic
  • You get 1 minute to prepare — take notes!
  • You speak for 1–2 minutes alone
  • The examiner may ask 1–2 follow-up questions
Part 3 · 4–5 min

Discussion

  • More abstract and analytical questions
  • Connected to the topic of Part 2
  • You must explain, compare, justify and discuss ideas in depth
Assessment

The 4 Criteria (each 25%)

1 · Fluency & Coherence
2 · Lexical Resource
3 · Grammar Range & Accuracy
4 · Pronunciation
Criterion 1 · 25%

Fluency & Coherence

  • Natural speed, no long pauses
  • Connect ideas with linking words
  • A pause in silence is OK — "um, uh" is NOT
  • Develop your answers; don't stop too soon
Criterion 2 · 25%

Lexical Resource (Vocabulary)

  • Use a wide range of vocabulary
  • Use collocations correctly (do homework, make a decision)
  • Paraphrase — don't repeat the same words
  • Use topic-specific words
Criterion 3 · 25%

Grammatical Range & Accuracy

  • Mix tenses: past, present, future
  • Complex sentences: conditionals, relative clauses, passives
  • Accuracy matters — but range matters too
  • Self-correct smoothly if you slip
Criterion 4 · 25%

Pronunciation

  • It's about being understandable — not having a British accent!
  • Word stress & sentence stress
  • Natural intonation (no flat robot voice!)
  • Clarity beats perfect sounds
Tip #1

Speak Naturally

❌ Weak
"I like reading."
✅ Band 7+
"I enjoy reading because it helps me relax after a busy day, and I especially like novels and biographies."
Tip #2

The Golden Formula

1 · ANSWER 2 · REASON 3 · EXAMPLE

Every answer = respond directly → explain why → give a detail or personal story.

Tip #3

Use Your 1-Minute Prep Well

  • What it was
  • When / Where
  • Details — what happened
  • Why it's memorable / how you felt

Tip #4

Never Stop in the Middle

✅ Smooth ending
"So that's why this memory is so special to me."

Silence hurts your Fluency score — a natural ending doesn't.

Tip #5

Buy Time Naturally

  • "Well, that's an interesting question…"
  • "Let me think for a second…"
  • "I've never thought about it before, but…"

Tip #7

Useful Linking Words

  • Adding: Moreover, In addition
  • Contrasting: However, Whereas
  • Sequencing: First of all, Then, Eventually
  • Examples: For instance, A case in point is
  • Concluding: All in all
🎮 Activity 4

Tip #8

Paraphrase, Don't Repeat

like → be fond of / enjoy / be into
important → crucial / essential / vital
very good → excellent / outstanding
think → believe / reckon / in my view
Tip #9

Mix Your Tenses 🕐

"I used to hate cooking, but now I really enjoy it, and I think I'll take a cooking class soon."

Past + Present + Future in one answer = Grammatical Range ✅

Tip #10 · Perfect for Part 3

Master Conditionals

"If governments invested more in public transport, cities would be cleaner."
"If I had the chance, I'd definitely live abroad."
Tip #11

Don't Memorize Answers! 🚫

  • Examiners spot memorized scripts immediately
  • Memorized answers → your score drops
  • Prepare ideas and vocabulary, not scripts
Tip #12

Slow Down! 🐢

Fast speaking ≠ fluent speaking.

Calm, clear speech = higher score ✅
